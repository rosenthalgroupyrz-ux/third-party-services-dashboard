### 1. Atlas Project Charter
Project Atlas is Northwind Logistics' expansion into Portugal, starting with a Lisbon distribution hub that opens in March 2027. The project is led by Joe Smith, with Maria Lopez on finance, Priya Natarajan on engineering, and Daniel Okafor on sales.

Success means 15 regional retail customers in the first six months and 94 percent on-time delivery within 90 days of launch.

### 2. Lisbon Hub Requirements
The Lisbon hub needs at least 2,500 square meters of floor space, loading bays for 15 vans, and a refrigerated area for cold-chain deliveries. It should be within 30 minutes of central Lisbon at morning peak.

Joe Smith will compare the three shortlisted warehouses against these requirements before the lease decision.

### 3. Atlas Budget Notes
Maria Lopez's working notes on the Project Atlas phase one budget. Total planned spend is 825,000 EUR: hub lease 240,000, vehicles and equipment 310,000, hiring and training 180,000, and marketing 95,000.

A 10 percent contingency is being added for fuel and currency swings. Break-even is forecast for month 14.

### 4. Driver Onboarding Guide
New Project Atlas drivers complete two weeks of paid training before their first solo route. Week one covers safety, vehicle checks and the delivery app; week two is ride-along shifts on real Lisbon routes.

Priya Natarajan owns the scheduling tool, and drivers' questions go to the hub manager.

### 5. Porto Interview Summary
Daniel Okafor interviewed five Porto retailers in October 2026. All five want same-day delivery for orders placed before 14:00, and four want tracking links in Portuguese and English.

Two grocery retailers need refrigerated delivery, which supports the cold-chain plan in the Atlas budget.

### 6. Route Optimizer Design Notes
The route optimizer groups delivery stops by neighborhood, keeps refrigerated stops on dedicated routes, and respects the 14:00 same-day cutoff. Early tests on sample Lisbon addresses cut average route time by about 12 percent.

Priya Natarajan's target is 15 percent before the March soft launch.

### 7. Competitor Landscape: Iberia
Three fictional competitors matter for Project Atlas: Costa Rapid (broad coverage, weak same-day), Tagus Freight (cheap, poor tracking) and Iberia Express Group (premium, high minimum volumes).

Daniel Okafor recommends competing on real-time tracking and cold-chain delivery rather than on price.

### 8. Soft Launch Plan
The Project Atlas soft launch in March 2027 starts with five retail customers chosen by Daniel Okafor. The team reviews delivery performance every day for the first four weeks.

Joe Smith makes the go or no-go call at the end of February, based on hub readiness, trained drivers, and confirmed customers.

### 9. Cold-Chain Vehicle Comparison
Leasing a refrigerated van costs about 1,450 EUR per month; buying costs about 62,000 EUR. Leasing keeps cash free during the first year and makes it easier to add vans if grocery demand grows.

Maria Lopez and Daniel Okafor will decide on October 28.

### 10. Atlas Risk Notes
The top three Project Atlas risks are hub lease delays, a driver hiring shortfall, and fuel or currency cost swings. Joe Smith reviews the risk register every Monday.

Mitigations include a short-term lease option, recruiting from November, and a 10 percent budget contingency from Maria Lopez.
